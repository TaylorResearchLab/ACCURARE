Scientific questions

Study disease through the biology of development.

Developmental gene regulation provides a framework for investigating both inherited disorders and childhood cancer. ACCURARE aims to connect genomic evidence with the anatomical, cellular, and temporal context in which genes act.

01

Shared genes and mechanisms

Investigate genes implicated in developmental disorders and pediatric tumors. Distinguish germline disease associations from somatic cancer alterations.

02

Developmental expression

Compare embryonic, fetal, and pediatric gene expression across human tissues and nonhuman primate references.

03

Evidence-based discovery

Integrate disease phenotypes, molecular pathways, and publications to formulate testable biological hypotheses.

Future research application

ACCURARE Hypothesis Studio

A proposed AI research environment where investigators work with language models to develop, compare, and evaluate biological hypotheses using ACCURARE evidence.

  • Retrieve relevant gene, disease, and developmental evidence.
  • Generate competing mechanisms and measurable predictions.
  • Link every substantive claim to sources and data releases.
  • Export a structured record for independent evaluation.

Planned access: a reusable AI skill and an authenticated, read-only MCP connection. Researchers could connect compatible AI clients using their own accounts. A separate sponsored service may be offered later.

Origins and NIH research support

Careful integration. Reproducible discovery.

ACCURARE builds on the NIH Common Fund U24 research program Uncovering the Shared Genetic Origins of Childhood Cancer and Structural Birth Defects Through Enhanced Data Integration and Analysis with the CFDE Data Distillery Knowledge Graph. The award at Children's Hospital of Philadelphia investigated shared genomic features of childhood cancers and structural birth defects through the Common Fund Data Ecosystem (CFDE) Data Distillery Knowledge Graph.

The U24 focused on integrating Kids First cohort data and NCI Molecular Targets Project cancer genomics. The proposed analyses included evidence-weighted graph relationships, link prediction, embeddings, and graph neural networks. ACCURARE extends that foundation with pediatric tissue expression, embryonic and fetal reference data, nonhuman primate comparisons, and researcher-guided hypothesis generation.

NIH award: 1U24OD038422-01 (September 19, 2024 to August 31, 2025). Contact PI: Deanne M. Taylor. Other PIs: Sharon Diskin, Elizabeth Goldmuntz, and Jonathan C. Silverstein.
